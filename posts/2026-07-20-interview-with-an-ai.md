What's the fastest way to understand a new consultant? Ask them questions and listen to how they think.

That's exactly what this video does with an AI. Not a benchmark, not a demo, an interview. Because the best way to build intuition for these tools isn't reading about them. It's sitting one down and taking a history.

{{< video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EdAjWXjnDcg >}}

## Interviewing an AI is history-taking

You already have this skill. A good history isn't a checklist; it's a strategy. Open-ended first, then focused. You probe inconsistencies. You ask the same question two ways and compare answers. You know the patient who speaks in confident complete sentences isn't necessarily the reliable historian.

Every one of those instincts transfers to a language model:

**Open broad, then narrow.** Start with "explain how you'd approach X" before "what's the dose of Y." You learn more from reasoning than from facts.

**Ask it to show its work.** "Walk me through your reasoning step by step" is the AI equivalent of asking a resident to present, not just conclude. Errors hide in conclusions and reveal themselves in reasoning.

**Challenge it.** Ask "what's the strongest argument against what you just said?" A useful consultant can argue the other side. So can a good model, and watching it do so shows you where the evidence is actually thin.

**Re-ask in a different form.** If rephrasing the question changes the answer, you've learned something important about how much to trust the first answer.

## The confident historian problem

Here's the trap, and it's the one thing to carry out of the interview: a language model is fluent even when it's wrong. It will state a fabricated citation with the same calm authority as a real one. In medicine we have a name for data that arrives with unearned confidence: we call it unverified, and we check it before it goes in the chart.

Treat AI output like a preliminary read. Useful, fast, often right, and signed by nobody. You are the final read.

## Try it yourself

Open any AI assistant tonight and run a five-minute interview on a topic you know cold, something from your own specialty. Ask it to explain, to reason step by step, to argue against itself, and to cite sources. Then check the citations.

Grading it on your home turf teaches you more about its reliability than any headline benchmark, because you can see exactly where fluency parted ways with accuracy.

## Recap

You don't need a computer science course to evaluate an AI. You need the skill you use every day: take a history, probe the reasoning, distrust unearned confidence, verify before you sign. Watch the interview, then go conduct your own.
